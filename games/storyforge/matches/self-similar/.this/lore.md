# lore — self-similar

## World / places / characters

**Halloway-Kest Tower** — a 41-story corporate office tower, downtown, unremarkable by day. After a certain hour, past a certain floor, the elevator no longer returns you to the lobby. It returns you to a floor that is the 41st floor again, but scaled, rotated, and warped by a consistent transformation -- smaller ceilings, a slightly acuter corner angle, the reception desk's laminate a shade darker, the same dead ficus repeated in a spiral of ever-smaller pots down the hall. This is not a maze. A maze has no rule. This has a rule.

- **The Custodian** — Voice: precise, unhurried, faintly archaic diction ("one does not *leave* a floor, one *maps out of it*"), speaks like a proof being read aloud. Never raises his voice, never seems surprised. Encountered at recursion depths that are powers the player can eventually predict. He is not a person so much as a personification of the transformation rule itself -- he knows things about Ellis he could not know, because he *is*, in some sense, made of the same repeated pattern Ellis is made of. He is not malicious; he is indifferent the way a math fact is indifferent. He answers direct, well-posed questions honestly and precisely; he has no patience for vague ones ("he does not deal in feelings, only in quantities").
- **The smaller one** — an echo of Ellis around age 8, found on a deeper (smaller-scale) floor, in a school-uniform version of the tower's lobby. Voice: genuinely a child's cadence, run-on sentences, guileless -- but says one or two things a child could not know, verbatim things Ellis said to himself at 8 that he never told anyone. Unsettling precisely because the innocence is real, not a mask.
- **The tall one** — an echo of Ellis around age 60+, found on a shallower (larger-scale) floor. Voice: weary, spare, clipped sentences, has stopped explaining himself to anyone including himself. Knows exactly how this goes, which is its own kind of horror -- he is not a guide, he is a warning that has already given up being one.
- **Priya Osei-Fontaine** (colleague, off-site, contactable only when Ellis is near a working phone line/floor) — Voice: warm, code-switches into French mid-sentence when stressed ("écoute, Ellis, I need you to actually listen to me for once") -- Montreal background. Represents the tether to the ordinary, un-recursed world; her half of any call is always slightly delayed/garbled, plausibly deniable as a bad signal, never confirmed as anything supernatural itself.
- **Environmental texture** — old memos, a security desk sign-in sheet, an emergency placard, occasionally in Spanish/Portuguese/Italian (a multinational tenant roster over the tower's history) -- flavor and unease, never load-bearing for a puzzle.

## Disruptive concept (learning goal)

**Concept:** Fractal geometry & iterated function systems (IFS) -- self-similarity, fractal dimension, attractors, and the fixed point of a recursive map. Taught at `reality_check: 10` rigor: this is not "spooky infinite hallway," it is a specific, nameable mathematical object, and Ellis can in principle predict, measure, and exploit it.

**Framing (adult-facing, no kid layer needed here):** the tower is generating copies of one floor plan through a repeated transformation (shrink, rotate a few degrees, skew, redecorate slightly) -- exactly how an IFS generates a fractal (the way the Sierpiński triangle is three shrunk copies of itself, forever). Depth doesn't mean "further away," it means "one more application of the transformation." Ellis is not walking a corridor; he is iterating a function.

**Vocabulary to introduce, in order:** self-similarity → scaling ratio → iteration / recursion depth → fractal dimension (why the stairwells feel like they violate ordinary 3D volume -- because locally they do) → attractor (the shape the iteration is converging toward, no matter where you start) → fixed point (the one input the transformation maps to itself -- the thing that doesn't change no matter how many times you apply the rule).

**Ladder:**
- **Rung 1 (Acts I-II, turns 1-7) — intuition & the wrong model.** Ellis assumes: dream, gas leak, prank, or (once scared enough) haunting/trap -- classic liminal-space genre logic, an antagonist with intent. He treats the space as *random* and tries genre-standard tactics (retrace steps, find the "real" exit, don't split the party -- there's no party). These fail in specific, informative ways (retracing steps lands him one iteration *deeper*, not back).
- **Rung 2 (Act III, turns 8-14) — the world contradicts the model, real mechanics taught.** The setback: trying to "just leave" via the stairwell costs him something (see Open threads) and does not work -- proof the naive model is wrong. Ellis starts *measuring* instead of fleeing: pacing off the reception desk, timing the ficus-pot spiral, noticing the scaling ratio is constant (~0.62, close to but not quite the golden ratio -- a deliberate real-feeling imperfection). He starts predicting where the Custodian will be, which floors will have a working phone line, which stairwell iteration will be "shallower." This is the training montage: he is learning to do fractal geometry under duress.
- **Rung 3 (Act V, turn 19) — the real concept, applied.** Escape is not found by distance (you cannot outwalk a recursion) and not by force. It is found by locating the **fixed point** -- the one floor-state that maps to itself under the transformation -- and consciously choosing to change what he does *at* that point rather than repeat the input he's fed the function every time before (see Established facts: the fixed point is a specific real decision from his life, not a room). Applying the real math (find the fixed point of the map, then change the map's input there) is what breaks the loop.

## Established facts
*(append here as the game commits to them; never silently contradict)*
- The elevator's 41→41 loop only triggers after 9pm and only when G is alone in the car.
- The scaling ratio between successive floors is consistently ~0.62 (measurable, not exact golden ratio -- a deliberate tell that this is engineered/generated, not "magic"). Confirmed in play (turn 3): the "preview" ficus on floor 0 matches the native ficus on floor 1 exactly in scale.
- **Refinement (turn 3):** the recursive transformation completes on *crossing a floor's threshold* (stepping onto its reception carpet), not merely on descending a stairwell flight to its landing. The stairwell itself is neutral/shared -- you can look through a landing door's window, or reach in from a doorway without stepping across, without triggering anything. This is what let G safely go back upstairs after only peeking at floor 1.
- **Time diverges independently of scale (turn 3):** floor-to-floor scale is a fixed, consistent ratio, but elapsed wear/time is *not* in sync between iterations -- the floor-1 ficus is the same size as its floor-0 preview but visibly older (browner, dustier). Whatever generates this is not instantaneous copying; each depth has its own independent history.
- **No spatial shortcut through the stairwell (turn 4):** G ran an ethernet cable from his floor-0 desk leg down to floor 1 to test a hypothesis that the stairwell is a higher-dimensional vertex with hidden adjacencies. Refuted directly and measurably -- the cable used exactly the ordinary physical length of the walked path, no more, no less. The recursion folds the *floor plan*, not the connecting stairwell, and there is no shortcut between depths.
- **A previous pass already ran this exact test (turn 4):** at floor 1's threshold, G found a second ethernet cable already coiled and secured to the equivalent desk-leg spot, tied off in a distinctive double-hitch -- the specific, slightly unusual knot G's father Walt taught him, which G ties automatically without thinking about it. G tied his cable to the existing one, joining them. Not yet revealed to G as anything more than "someone else's cable" -- the personal-knot recognition is a deniable, dismissible detail *to him*, load-bearing to the player.
- Retracing your exact prior path (once a threshold has actually been *crossed*) always lands one iteration *deeper*, never back -- the map has no inverse from outside a fixed point. (Not yet demonstrated in play -- reserved for the Act III setback.)
- The Custodian appears at floors whose depth is a power of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16...) -- a fact the player can learn to predict and exploit. Not yet encountered.
- **Act IV reveal, not yet delivered:** this is not the first time G has done this. There are faint traces (the pot's disturbed soil, the coffee ring, the pre-tied cable in his own knot style) that this recursion has run before -- possibly many times -- and the fixed point is not a place. It's the decision G makes every year to tell himself he'll visit his father "next time," handed back to him at every scale.
