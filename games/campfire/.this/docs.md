# campfire — docs

The world, the cast, and the opening scene. This is the narrator's reference, not a script — bend it to follow the player's choices.

## The world: Honeywood

A cozy little village tucked in a friendly forest. Round doors, warm windows, chimneys puffing. The folk of Honeywood are gentle animals and small mischievous helpers. Nothing here is truly dangerous — the worst you'll meet is a grumpy badger who just needs a nap, or a river that wants to be crossed cleverly.

Tone: warm, whimsical, funny, a little bit magical. Bedtime-story cozy.

## The hook

Last night a **little star fell out of the sky** and landed somewhere in the forest. It's small, glowing, and a bit lost — it hums when it's scared and giggles when it's happy. The village asks the hero to help the star find its way back home to the night sky before it gets too dim.

## The cast

| Who | What they're like |
|-----|-------------------|
| **The hero** | The player. Let the kids pick the name and whether they're a kid, a fox, a bunny — whatever they like. Brave and kind. |
| **The sidekick** | A small companion the hero picks up early. Let the kids name it too. Could be a sleepy hedgehog, a chatty firefly, a wobbly duckling. |
| **The little star** | Glows warm, hums when nervous, giggles when happy. Wants to go home. Can light up dark places as a "power." |
| **Old Nana Owl** | Wise, kind, a bit forgetful. Knows how to send a star home — but needs three things gathered first. |
| **Bramble the badger** | Grumpy only because he's tired. A soft heart under the grump. |

## The gentle quest shape

To send the star home, Nana Owl needs three simple things gathered around the forest. Each is a tiny, kid-sized puzzle or choice — no fighting, just cleverness and kindness:

1. **A drop of moon-dew** — from the top of the Big Hill (how do we get up? climb, ask a bird for a lift, build steps?).
2. **A song** — the star flies home on a tune. Someone in the forest knows it (find who; maybe Bramble hums it in his sleep).
3. **A safe launch spot** — the old stone well, the tallest tree, or the quiet meadow. The player decides where to say goodbye.

Gather the three, help the star home, warm happy ending. The whole thing should feel like it can wrap in one sitting, or stretch across several if the kids are having fun.

## The opening scene (start here on a fresh game)

> The sun is setting over Honeywood, painting the little rooftops gold. You're just finishing supper when there's a soft *tap-tap-tap* at your round front door. You open it — and there, sitting on your doorstep, is a **tiny glowing star**, no bigger than an apple. It hums a small, worried hum and blinks up at you.

Then ask the player (the kids) two quick things before offering the first choices:
- **Who are you?** (name + are you a kid, a fox, a bunny, something else?)
- Then, first choices: *Do you (a) scoop the star up gently, (b) kneel down and say hello first, or (c) fetch it a little blanket?* — and always allow "or something else."

Keep beats short. Offer 2–4 choices each time plus a free option. Bend everything toward wonder.
